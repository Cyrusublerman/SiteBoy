INITIAL EXPERIMENTS
How to convert phyllotaxis into a dome?
Plants grow from the center out, with
their leaves being phyllotactically
distributed and spread based on
growth order and time. This, if
simplified creates a dome with each
leaf creating an outer point at a
specific location.
Naturally however, the leaves
will occupy the majority of space
between the central axis and the
outer point. This would prevent most
functional use inside the dome if
recreated architecturally and thus
is an inadequate translation of the
geometry. The question to answer
then is “how can the outer points
be distributed in accordance with
phyllotaxy without the structure
occupying the inner area?”
To the right are renderings of several
visual experiments in which different
avenues were explored. Firstly is the
consequence of forming a low polygon
spiral that was a simplification of the
golden spiral. This was then cloned
and rotated to create a variance of
frameworks that united curves into
domes. These domes could in turn
have an outer layer attached to further
solidify and exaggerate the shape.
These domes, though using the golden
angle, do not capture the aesthetics
of plant growth and when mapped
against the points of phyllotaxy, only
overlap with a small subset of points
suggesting that using the golden angle
and simple repetition and rotational
symmetry would not be enough.
Investigation into the potential
mechanisms of supporting structures
however indicated that there may
be potential in some aspects of
this design, such as the interplay
between overlapping spirals. Further
investigation was needed.
