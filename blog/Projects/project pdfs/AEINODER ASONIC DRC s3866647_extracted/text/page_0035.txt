Absorption
A material’s ability to absorb sound is measured with the sound absorbtion
coefficient. This is the percentage of the sound wave’s energy that is
transferred to the material. This coefficient is related to the frequencies of the
sound and the incidence direction of the sound (Dunne, Desai & Sadiku 2017).
The efficiency of sound absorption is related to a series of factors:
1. The material’s mass density: The material’s mass density is inversely
correlated to the preferred frequency of absorption. The higher the mass
density, the higher the absorption of low frequencies and lower the
absorption of high frequencies (Abdi et al. 2021).
2. The thickness of a material: Higher thickness is associated with higher
absorption of lower frequencies of sound. It has however, low impact on
the absorption of higher frequencies (Koruk & Genc 2018).
3. The features of a material’s pores: Smaller pores in higher number will
yield the greatest sound absorption.
