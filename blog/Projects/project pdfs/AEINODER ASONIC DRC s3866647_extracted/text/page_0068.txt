Cellulose Fibre
Cellulose fibre is a common insulative material often used
in house insulation, treating attics and floors, It’s popularity
and proliferation is rising due to being one of the most
sustainable options for insulation (Arenas et al. 2014).
Cellulose fibre can be created from a multitude of sources
including recycled paper and food waste. At the product’s
end of life, the cellulose fibre can be recycled into more
cellulose-based products or it can be degraded into simple,
eco-friendly compounds (Li et al. 2014).
The properties that facilitate cellulose fibre excellence
in heat insulation also lend themselves to the process of
sound absorbance. The porosity of the structure force
encountered sound-waves to transfer their energy, as they
travel through varying molecular densities. This vibration
of the pores converts the energy into heat and physical
movement, lessening the remaining energy of the sound
(Zhang et al. 2014).
The porous cellulose fibre performs optimally in typical loud
anthrophonic environments with thicknesses >50mm (see
right). Increasing this thickness further does increase the
absorbance of lower frequencies. This paradigm may be
exploited in the formation of sound mediation structures if
needed (Arenas et al. 2014).
