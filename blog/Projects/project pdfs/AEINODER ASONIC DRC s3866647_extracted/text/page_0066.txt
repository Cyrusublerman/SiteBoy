Poly(3-hydroxybutyrate-co-3-hydroxyvalerate)(PHBV)
A plastic in the family of polyhydroxyalkanoates (PHAs), poly(3-
hydroxybutyrate-co-3-hydroxyvalerate) (PHBV) has been the focus of manifold
research. The structural and physicochemical properties of PHBV have proven
to be of high value in many industries including medicine (Tebaldi et al. 2019).
PHBV is much less prone to biodegradation than other PHA biopolymers
and can be synthesised to have more hydrophobic properties. Though this
is often seen as a negative composition when purposed for specific medical
applications such as cell interaction through drug therapy, this is of high benefit
when creating an object that will need to withstand microbial proliferation
within a hospital environment (Figueroa-Lopez et al. 2020.
PHBV may be synthesised within the cytoplasm of both gram-negative and
gram-positive bacteria. Each of these bacterial groups, however, pose their own
issues. Synthesis of PHBV by gram-positive bacteria have proven to be far more
costly as the purification processes are excessively difficult. Gram-negative
bacterial synthesis can lead to the co-production of specific endotoxins that
that may cause inflammatory reactions. This potential toxic reaction may not be
an issue when the material is used in the creation of a non-ingested product.
