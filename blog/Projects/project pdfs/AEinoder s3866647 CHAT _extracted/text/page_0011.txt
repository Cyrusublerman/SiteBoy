The prosodic features of language can shape
the words and sentences, altering their
meaning and importance to the listener
(Hunston 2007). By altering the rhythm and
stressing of syllables the communicator can
alter the tone of the speech, incorporating
emotion or contextual elements such as
sarcasm or focus (Cheang & Pell 2008;
Mehndiratta, Sachdeva & Soni 2017).
Gestural actions differ from other physical
forms of nonverbal communication. They
are a means of conveying a wide variety
of information and feelings, allowing
movements of the body to communicate
their own messages or assist verbal speech
- emphasising and modifying the meaning
(Iverson & Goldin-Meadow 2005).
