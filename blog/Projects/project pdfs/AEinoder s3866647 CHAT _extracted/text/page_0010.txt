N O N - V E R B A L
C O M M U N I C A T I O N
The ways in which a person moves
and uses their body can convey
and communicate emotions and
information (Marmpena, Lim & Dahl
2018).
The meaning and intentions of speech
can vary heavily based on aspects such as
eye contact, posture, gestures, and other
forms of body language (Kleinke 1986). In
addition to these actions, communication
can also be further modulated and
expanded with alterations in distance (also
known as proxemics), interactions with
objects and the environment, and the use
of touch (haptics)(Pennycook 1985).
The speech itself also has within it key
aspects of non-verbal communication.
Non-verbal voice aspects (paralanguage)
as well as the use of time (chronemics)
allow for the spacing of words, expression
of thought processes, and provision of
insight on the level of consideration of the
speaker (Walther & Tidwell 1995).
Paralanguage is an incredibly important
part of speech (James 2017). By altering
the pitch, volume, and intonation the
user can add depth to the meaning of the
words used in conversation. The use of
paralanguage can be both conscious and
subconscious and can even exist within
handwriting and sign language in an
altered form (Tsonos & Kouroupetroglou
2008).
